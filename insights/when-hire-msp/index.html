When should a business hire an MSP?

The answer is usually visible in the cost of interruptions, unclear ownership, and decisions that keep getting deferred.

Hire an MSP when technology has become operationally important enough to need an owner, but not necessarily large enough to justify a full internal team.

Five signals

  1. People lose time waiting for answers or working around recurring issues.
  2. No one can confidently explain your backup, access, or security posture.
  3. Onboarding and offboarding depend on one person remembering every step.
  4. Leadership is pulled into vendor, renewal, and outage decisions.
  5. The business is growing, moving offices, adding locations, or adopting new systems.

What to expect from the first 90 days

A good MSP should build an accurate inventory, address urgent risks, establish response norms, and propose a short prioritized roadmap. You should feel more informed—not locked into more complexity.

Ask how they measure progress and how you can leave the relationship if it stops working.
